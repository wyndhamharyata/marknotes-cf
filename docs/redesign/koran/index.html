Proposal only. No application code changed. Nothing under src/, config, the Durable Object, Cloudflare or production was touched, and nothing was deployed.

This report is docs/redesign/koran/index.html. Its assets sit beside it: thumbnails and heroes scaled down from src/assets/ and src/content/blog/hero-images/, the GitHub avatar from AUTHOR_AVATAR, a photo from the Banjarnegara article, Couvee’s photos from Kopimap, and two crops of Kopimap’s printed map (kopimap_print_light.json and _dark.json) at Couvee Solo for the ticket. Titles, dates, ratings, the Couvee shop and its two comments are real, read on 29 October 2026 from the repo, https://mwyndham.dev and https://kopimap.mwyndham.dev/api/coffees (21 articles, 3 reviews). Every number, chart and moderated comment in section 10 is illustrative.

The design language is Nota as it shipped on Kopimap: the same tokens, materials, type, shape rules and motion. Only the objects are new.

Marknotes becomes the newspaper on the same counter. At a warung kopi the koran lies on the counter for whoever sits down, and Kopimap’s review clipping is cut from it. It lies on a terrazzo counter. The front page is a broadsheet, the archive is a run of back pages, each article is a sheet of newsprint, a coffee review has its shop’s ticket stapled to it, and a comment is written on the pink carbon nota and filed as a thermal slip. Prints and code are in one ink until you point at them.

Changes in this pass

  • Colour balances the texture. Every grey still measures neutral (equal red, green and blue), but grain on plain grey reads as old paper. Grey surfaces now carry colour from the palette: the newsprint has blue, red, mint and orange fibres, the counter under the page is terrazzo with chips in the palette colours, and reviews carry a mint ticket stub.
  • Real newsprint. The sheet’s texture is fine grain, dark specks, light inclusions and short fibres, replacing the soft mottle.
  • Prints and code come to colour. Halftone prints show their colour photo under the pointer. Code is one ink at rest and shows its syntax colours under the pointer.
  • A new Blog page. The receipt is gone. Blog is the paper’s back pages: one band per year, the year in big numerals, and the stories set in columns with their prints.
  • Reviews say they are coffee shop reviews. The chop reads COFFEE REVIEW, and the shop’s mint ticket stub, with its name and cups, is stapled to the print.
  • Night inside the article. Desktop night for the review, its ticket and map, code and comments, under a lamp.
  • Admin, every page. Home, Articles, an article’s numbers, the editor and moderation, plus moderation on a phone. daisyUI now leaves the whole site.

1. What the current UI is, read from the code

  • It is the stock daisyUI blog. Every page is a card bg-base-100 shadow-xl (src/layouts/BlogPost.astro:17, src/pages/index.astro, src/pages/articles/index.astro) on a dotted engineering grid with a blue wash (src/layouts/BaseLayout.astro:23-24). The nav is a floating rounded bar (src/pages/_components/Header.astro:7) whose current page is a blue btn btn-accent pill (HeaderLink.astro). Nothing on screen is about coffee, Solo or the person writing, and Kopimap, one click away, now looks like another product.
  • Three emphasis colours, and one changes hue at night. ARTICLE_PROSE_CLASS (src/consts.ts:5-6) makes bold primary green, italic secondary and links accent blue, then corrects all three with brightness filters. Primary is green by day (src/styles/global.css:23) and coral red by night (global.css:59), so the same bold word changes hue with the OS. The dark theme is daisyUI’s navy “sunset” base (global.css:54-55), not a neutral.
  • Every date is a day early. FormattedDate.astro:11 calls toLocaleDateString with no time zone. pubDate: "Jun 22 2025" is parsed as midnight on a UTC+7 machine, stored as 2025-06-21T17:00:00.000Z, and printed in UTC. The live Couvee page says Jun 21, 2025, and Building Keling says May 23, 2026 against May 24 in its file. This is a one-line fix, timeZone: "Asia/Jakarta", and it is step 1 below.
  • Reviews look like every other post. 3 of 21 articles are coffee reviews with a rating, but the list (ArticleThumbnail.astro:22-32) gives all of them the same rounded 128px photo, title and date. The rating only appears inside the shop block, below the fold.
  • The shop block speaks a different language from Kopimap. src/components/ShopBlock.tsx draws daisyUI stars in accent blue (:66-75), soft success and warning badges (:96-100), a green and a blue outline button, and a stock MapLibre teardrop (:36) on the alidade_smooth style (:30). On Kopimap the same shop is a mint ticket with a red Reviewed chop, a cup card and a square stamp on the printed map.
  • The hero is a 256px strip. max-h-64 object-cover (BlogPost.astro:26) crops every hero to a band: GORM’s hero is a code screenshot cut mid-line, Keling’s is three app screens cut at the waist.
  • The headline is a bigger body. The title is Atkinson at 36px, centred (BlogPost.astro:44), and section heads use the same face. Nothing marks where an article starts except size.
  • Comments are small and tinted. The message is set at 10pt (CommentItem.tsx:38), a third smaller than the article. Replies sit on bg-accent/5, /10, /15 rounded tints (:60-66). Identicons are full-hue (Avatar.tsx:13) in squircle masks (:16), and Author is a green soft pill.
  • Floating circles on the text. Two btn-circle buttons, green and black, sit fixed bottom-right on every article (ScrollButtons.astro:9,18) and cover line ends on phones.
  • Admin is in the visitor’s menu (Header.astro:39,56), and admin itself is a second daisyUI kit: stat tiles, a glass dock with backdrop-blur, join button groups, status as coloured left borders and soft badges (CommentFilter.astro, CommentModerationItem.astro), and lines that explain the title above them (admin/index.astro:28-30, StatSnapshot.astro).
  • Rounded everything. --radius-selector: 2rem, --radius-field: 1rem, --radius-box: .5rem (global.css:39-41), plus rounded-box and rounded-lg on thumbnails, photos and replies.

2. The direction: Koran

Koran is Indonesian for newspaper. It is the paper object every warung kopi already has, so Marknotes joins Kopimap on the same counter instead of borrowing a second metaphor. One source of objects keeps it coherent; anything with no object stays plain type.

The uncommon patterns

  • One ticket, two sites. A review’s shop block is Kopimap’s ticket itself: the same card, chop, cups, dotted facts and printed map, stapled to the top of the review. On Home and Blog the same shop rides as a stub, like a stub on Kopimap’s rail. Kopimap clips the review; Marknotes staples the ticket.
  • Prints come to colour. Every photo is printed in halftone until the pointer is on it, and then it is the colour photo. Code is one ink until the pointer is on it, and then it has its syntax colours. The page stays calm, and colour arrives where you look.
  • Colour balances texture. Wherever a grey surface carries grain, it also carries a little colour from the palette, so the grain reads as paper, not age.
  • Pink and blue mean you are writing. The comment box and the article editor are Kopimap’s edit nota: pink carbonless paper, ruled lines, your words in biro.
  • The chop is a signature. Red ink means one thing on both sites: Wyndham marked this.

To be honest, the coffee selection is not that much. It only has “house blend” and has no access to various specialty coffees, and the taste of the coffee is fine, albeit it lands on the sour side of the spectrum.

6. Desktop: code, lists and photos

GORM Maximalism, from its first section. The pointer is on the second printout, so it shows its colours.

Why?

Using GORM for your production code? still querying models one by one like a cave man? tired of yet another repository method that spell db.Find(&result).Error? This blogpost is for you. This is the collection of many neat tricks that GORM can do where I seldomly seen being used in the wild.

Trick 1: Preloading

Imagine you have two models that’s related (1-to-many):

type User struct {
  gorm.Model
  Name        string
  Email       string
}

type Report struct {
  gorm.Model
  Score       uint
  UserID      uint
}

And you want to fetch user data, but also returning report all at once. Doing it this way is the noob way:

var user User
err := db.Find(&user,1).Error
// ...
var reports []Report
err := db.Where("user_id = ?", 1).Find(&reports).Error

Instead, you can declare that User have many Reports and having it preloaded immediately:

var user User
err := db.Preload("Reports").Find(&user,1).Error

Neat!

  1. Code is a printout on thermal paper, torn at both ends. At rest it is one ink: keywords bold, comments in ink-2 italic. Under the pointer it shows its syntax colours. Built with Shiki’s two-theme output: a one-ink theme as the default and a colour theme in --shiki-colour, swapped by one CSS rule.
  2. Inline code is a thermal chip with a 1px ink frame.
  3. Lists use square ink bullets; ordered lists keep their numbers and today’s alpha and roman nesting.
  4. Photos (BlogImage) are already colour: a print with a white border and black photo corners. A caption, when the article gives one, is mono under the print.

And yet, the same drug store located at the intersection in the market of Banjarnegara, where people barely visit, still standing strong for all I’ve been alive and able to remember anything.

I assume it generates just enough revenue to go by, and all the thing that keep it alive is just “that’s how it always been”.

Nothing is set below 12px. The display face is for names, headlines and numbers, never for body sentences.

Shape rules

  • No radius, no circles. *, ::before, ::after { border-radius: 0 }, as on Kopimap. Bullets, LEDs, identicons, staples and plot points are squares. Curves only appear in type, photos, logos, fibres and map geometry.
  • Four edges. Straight cut for newsprint and card. Torn, 14px teeth, for thermal paper only. Perforated, 4px dashes, for the reply line, stubs and ledger heads. Double frame for stamps.
  • Rotation is for ink. Stamps turn between −4° and +4°, seeded from the slug or comment id. Paper lies straight.
  • Rules. 1px ink at 35–50% between columns and stories. 1.5px for the dateline and Comments head. 3px above each year and ### head, 5px above ##, 6px plus 1.5px under the masthead and flag.
  • Shadows. Paper: a 1px contact line and a soft drop. Keys: a 2px hard bottom and a 5px soft drop. Light from the top-left.
  • Targets. Keys 40px on desktop, 44–46px on phones. Ledger rows 72px.

16. Risks

  • Kitsch. A newspaper costume turns into a theme park fast: fake bylines, kickers, “Vol. 1 No. 21” and fold lines. None of those are here. The guard is Kopimap’s: one source of objects, every colour one job, and anything with no object stays plain type.
  • A textured page for long reading. The fibres and specks are sparse and small, and ink holds 13.43:1 on the flat colour. If a 2,400-word article reads busy, the fibres drop to half density first; the sheet can move to thermal white last.
  • Scroll cost on low-end Android. Kopimap found full-page SVG-turbulence backgrounds expensive. The newsprint tile is SVG noise plus drawn fibres; if scrolling lags, it becomes one 300px PNG tile (about 40 KB), the same fix planned for Kopimap.
  • Colour on hover only reaches mice. On touch, list prints stay halftone; only an article’s hero answers a tap. Body photos are colour anyway.
  • Fonts. Big Shoulders, the Stencil subset and Atkinson Mono add about 60–80 KB woff2. Pages are prerendered, so this is the only new first-load cost.
  • Copied CSS drifts. The Nota classes live in two repos. A comment at the top of each block naming the other file is the cheap guard.

17. Decisions I need from you

  1. Article paper: newsprint with fibres (recommended), or thermal white for long reading.
  2. Night switch: in the colophon (recommended), or a fifth key in the till.
  3. Admin link: a key in the colophon (recommended), removed entirely (you type /admin), or kept in the till.
  4. The date fix ships now on its own, ahead of the redesign (recommended).
