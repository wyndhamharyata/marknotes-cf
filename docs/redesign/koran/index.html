Marknotes becomes the newspaper on the same counter. At a warung kopi the koran lies on the counter for whoever sits down, and Kopimap’s review clipping is cut from it. The front page is a broadsheet, the archive is a receipt printed from the till, each article is a sheet of newsprint, a coffee review has its shop’s ticket stapled to it, and a comment is written on the pink carbon nota and filed as a thermal slip. 1. What the current UI is, read from the code

  • It is the stock daisyUI blog. Every page is a card bg-base-100 shadow-xl (src/layouts/BlogPost.astro:17, src/pages/index.astro, src/pages/articles/index.astro) on a dotted engineering grid with a blue wash (src/layouts/BaseLayout.astro:23-24). The nav is a floating rounded bar (src/pages/_components/Header.astro:7) whose current page is a blue btn btn-accent pill (HeaderLink.astro). Nothing on screen is about coffee, Solo or the person writing, and Kopimap, one click away, now looks like another product.
  • Three emphasis colours, and one changes hue at night. ARTICLE_PROSE_CLASS (src/consts.ts:5-6) makes bold primary green, italic secondary and links accent blue, then corrects all three with brightness filters. Primary is green by day (src/styles/global.css:23) and coral red by night (global.css:59), so the same bold word changes hue with the OS. The dark theme is daisyUI’s navy “sunset” base (global.css:54-55), not a neutral.
  • Every date is a day early. FormattedDate.astro:11 calls toLocaleDateString with no time zone. pubDate: "Jun 22 2025" is parsed as midnight on a UTC+7 machine, stored as 2025-06-21T17:00:00.000Z, and printed in UTC. The live Couvee page says Jun 21, 2025, and Building Keling says May 23, 2026 against May 24 in its file. This is a one-line fix, timeZone: "Asia/Jakarta", and it is step 1 below.
  • Reviews look like every other post. 3 of 21 articles are coffee reviews with a rating, but the list (ArticleThumbnail.astro:22-32) gives all of them the same rounded 128px photo, title and date. The rating only appears inside the shop block, below the fold.
  • The shop block speaks a different language from Kopimap. src/components/ShopBlock.tsx draws daisyUI stars in accent blue (:66-75), soft success and warning badges (:96-100), a green and a blue outline button, and a stock MapLibre teardrop (:36) on the alidade_smooth style (:30). On Kopimap the same shop is a mint ticket with a red Reviewed chop, a cup card and a square stamp on the printed map.
  • The hero is a 256px strip. max-h-64 object-cover (BlogPost.astro:26) crops every hero to a band: GORM’s hero is a code screenshot cut mid-line, Keling’s is three app screens cut at the waist.
  • The headline is a bigger body. The title is Atkinson at 36px, centred (BlogPost.astro:44), and section heads use the same face. Nothing marks where an article starts except size.
  • Comments are small and tinted. The message is set at 10pt (CommentItem.tsx:38), a third smaller than the article. Replies sit on bg-accent/5, /10, /15 rounded tints (:60-66). Identicons are full-hue (Avatar.tsx:13) in squircle masks (:16), and Author is a green soft pill.
  • Floating circles on the text. Two btn-circle buttons, green and black, sit fixed bottom-right on every article (ScrollButtons.astro:9,18) and cover line ends on phones.
  • Admin is in the visitor’s menu. “Admin Panel” is the fifth link for everyone (Header.astro:39,56), and on phones the five links sit behind a hamburger dropdown.
  • Rounded everything. --radius-selector: 2rem, --radius-field: 1rem, --radius-box: .5rem (global.css:39-41), plus rounded-box and rounded-lg on thumbnails, photos and replies.

The uncommon patterns

  • One ticket, two sites. A review’s shop block is Kopimap’s ticket itself: the same card, chop, cups, dotted facts and printed map, stapled to the top of the review. Kopimap clips the review; Marknotes staples the ticket. A reader moving between the two sites sees one object in both places.
  • The archive is a receipt. Blog feeds out of the till’s slot the way Kopimap’s list does: one row per article, the date sitting where the price would be, a chop and score on the three reviews and nothing on anything else. On desktop it prints in line by line with the raster reveal Kopimap already ships.
  • Pink and blue mean you are writing. The comment box is Kopimap’s edit nota: pink carbonless paper, ruled lines, your words in biro and your alias pre-printed on the From line. Once posted it becomes a thermal slip in ink, like everyone else’s, so what is yours and unsent can be told apart from across the room.
  • The chop is a signature. A chop is a personal seal. Red ink means one thing on both sites: Wyndham marked this. Reviewed on a shop, Author on a comment, and nowhere else.

Just this weekend, as I worked on the blog that you’re reading right now, I visited Couvee for the nth time this year.

Why? Well, because for whatever reason, either the vibe, the coffee, or the position of the table and chair always triggers my mood to be very productive.

The Coffee

To be honest, the coffee selection is not that much. It only has “house blend” and has no access to various specialty coffees, and the taste of the coffee is fine, albeit it lands on the sour side of the spectrum.

Other menu selections are also not that many. Several staples such as Americano, Latte, and Cappuccino, followed by some alternative options such as Americano with Lychee syrup, Latte with Manuka Honey, non-coffee options such as Chocolate and Matcha Latte, and some other menus I can’t remember on top of my head.

The Donut

Yep, you read that right. This small coffee shop, for whatever reason, shares space with another food brand: Kalis. Kalis is a donut brand, and this small spot houses different cashiers and all.

  1. The sheet is newsprint, 880px wide, body at 18/1.65 in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, about 70 characters a line.
  2. The hero is printed in halftone at 16:7 inside the margins, so a donut photo and a code screenshot both read as a newspaper print, and neither is cut to a 256px band (decision 2).
  3. The headline is Big Shoulders 900 at 70px, left-aligned and balanced. The dateline below it is mono between two rules: the date, Updated, and a Comments link that replaces the floating button.
  4. The ticket is Couvee’s, as Kopimap shows it: the name at 32px, the red Reviewed chop, the cup card and 4.0, Hours on a dotted leader with “open now”, and the address. Open in Kopimap is the orange key because it is the block’s job; Directions is steel. Two staples hold it to the sheet.
  5. The map is Kopimap’s printed style (kopimap_print_light.json on R2) with the shop’s chop stamp in crop marks, instead of alidade_smooth and a teardrop. It still loads only when the ticket scrolls into view.

6. Desktop: code, lists and photos

GORM Maximalism, from its first section. The sheet only; the till and hero are as above.

Why?

Using GORM for your production code? still querying models one by one like a cave man? tired of yet another repository method that spell db.Find(&result).Error? This blogpost is for you. This is the collection of many neat tricks that GORM can do where I seldomly seen being used in the wild.

Trick 1: Preloading

Imagine you have two models that’s related (1-to-many):

type User struct {
  gorm.Model
  Name        string
  Email       string
}

type Report struct {
  gorm.Model
  Score       uint
  UserID      uint
}

And you want to fetch user data, but also returning report all at once. Doing it this way is the noob way:

var user User
err := db.Find(&user,1).Error
// ...
var reports []Report
err := db.Where("user_id = ?", 1).Find(&reports).Error

Instead, you can declare that User have many Reports and having it preloaded immediately:

var user User
err := db.Preload("Reports").Find(&user,1).Error

Neat!

  1. Code is a printout on thermal paper, torn at both ends, in one ink: keywords bold, comments in ink-2 italic, nothing coloured. A thermal printer prints one colour, and colour on this site belongs to objects (decision 3). Long lines scroll inside the printout.
  2. Inline code is a thermal chip with a 1px ink frame, the same paper as the printouts.
  3. Lists use square ink bullets; ordered lists keep their numbers and today’s alpha and roman nesting.
  4. Photos (BlogImage) stay in colour: a print with a white border and black photo corners, as on Kopimap’s ticket. A caption, when the article gives one, is mono under the print.

And yet, the same drug store located at the intersection in the market of Banjarnegara, where people barely visit, still standing strong for all I’ve been alive and able to remember anything.

I assume it generates just enough revenue to go by, and all the thing that keep it alive is just “that’s how it always been”.

Shape rules

  • No radius, no circles. *, ::before, ::after { border-radius: 0 }, as on Kopimap. Bullets, LEDs, identicons and staples are squares. Curves only appear in type, photos, logos and map geometry.
  • Four edges. Straight cut for newsprint and card. Torn, 14px teeth, for thermal paper only: the receipt, slips and printouts. Perforated, 4px dashes, for the reply line and the receipt’s total. Double frame for stamps.
  • Rotation is for ink. Stamps turn between −4° and +4°, seeded from the article slug or comment id. Paper lies straight.
  • Rules. 1px ink at 45–50% between front-page columns. 1.5px ink for the dateline and Comments head. 3px and 5px ink above section heads. 6px plus 1.5px under the masthead.
  • Shadows. Paper: a 1px contact line and a soft drop. Keys: a 2px hard bottom and a 5px soft drop. Light from the top-left.
  • Targets. Keys 40px on desktop, 44–46px on phones. Receipt rows 74px.

16. Risks

  • Kitsch. A newspaper costume turns into a theme park fast: fake bylines, kickers, “Vol. 1 No. 21” and fold lines. None of those are here. The guard is the same as Kopimap’s: one source of objects, every colour one job, and anything with no object stays plain type.
  • A grey page for long reading. Newsprint is dimmer than today’s near-white card. Ink holds 13.43:1, but if a 2,400-word Go article reads tired, the sheet can move to thermal white without changing anything else (decision 1).
  • One-ink code. Syntax colour goes. Bold keywords and grey comments carry most of it; the fallback is Shiki’s colours on one dark plate, at the cost of a fifth paper (decision 3).
  • Scroll cost on low-end Android. Kopimap found full-page SVG-turbulence backgrounds expensive to rasterise. Here the counter and the sheet carry them over the whole page. If scrolling lags, the grain, mottle and brush become small tiled PNGs, the same fix planned for Kopimap.
  • Fonts. Big Shoulders, the Stencil subset and Atkinson Mono add about 60–80 KB woff2 over today’s single family. Pages are prerendered, so this is the only first-load cost.
  • Copied CSS drifts. The Nota classes live in two repos. A comment at the top of each block naming the other file is the cheap guard.
  • 360px phones. The four till keys fit at 13px; a 320px screen would need the Kopimap key to shorten.

17. Decisions I need from you

  1. Article paper: newsprint (recommended), or thermal white for long reading.
  2. Hero: halftone (recommended), or colour. Photos inside articles stay colour either way.
  3. Code: one-ink thermal printout (recommended), or Shiki colours on a dark plate.
  4. Night switch: in the colophon (recommended), or a fifth key in the till. Most readers never need it, since it follows the OS.
  5. Admin: tokens only in this round (recommended), or the full pass as step 8.
  6. Admin link: a key in the colophon (recommended), removed entirely (you type /admin), or kept in the till.
  7. The date fix ships now on its own, ahead of the redesign (recommended).
